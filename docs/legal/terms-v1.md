## 1. What this site is, and who runs it

xer-hero is a public library of Primavera P6 programmes. Planners upload `.xer` files; the
site parses them and publishes them, with a page per programme and the original file to
download.

It is run by one person — Carlo Greblo — in their own time. There is no company, no
support desk and no service level. The site is free to use and nothing on it is for sale.

Using this site means accepting these terms. If you do not accept them, do not use it.

## 3. What you promise when you upload

Every upload asks you to confirm three things, and every upload re-asserts all three:

1. You own the programme, or you have permission from whoever does to publish it.
2. It contains no confidential or commercially sensitive third-party information — and you
   understand that the file may name people, that it is published exactly as you uploaded
   it, and that nothing is stripped from it.
3. You licence it to everyone under CC-BY 4.0, **irrevocably**.

The third one is the sentence that matters when someone changes their mind: **the licence
cannot be withdrawn.** You may withdraw the programme; you cannot withdraw the licence
from copies and forks that have already been taken.

The version of these terms in force when you upload is recorded against that upload, and
it is linked from it.

## 4. The licence you grant

The licence covers the file itself and everything the site computes from it — the parsed

**The site takes no separate licence of its own.** There is no clause here granting the

## 5. Attribution, forks and revisions

- The site renders attribution from the lineage it holds. The `.xer` file is never
  modified: the format has no comment mechanism, and modifying a file would break its
  round-trip back into P6.
- Forking on the site satisfies CC-BY automatically. A fork's page renders the full
  ancestry chain, the licence, and the change note its uploader wrote.
- Reusing a programme off the site is your obligation, as with any CC-BY work. Every
  programme page carries a citation line to copy.
- Revisions of a programme may be uploaded by its owner only. Everyone else forks.

## 6. Withdrawing your own programme

You can withdraw a programme you uploaded yourself, at either granularity: one revision,
or the whole series. It is self-service and no operator is involved.

What happens: the page stays at its URL and says the programme was withdrawn by its
uploader; the file is deleted; the title and the Handle stay visible; and **forks are
untouched**, because the licence was validly granted when they were taken.

The deletion of the file is permanent. There is no undo.

## 7. Reports and removals

- Anyone may report a programme, with no account, at [the report page](/report).
- Removals for rights, confidentiality or personal-data reasons **cascade to everything
  forked from the removed content**, because the licence was never validly granted in the
  first place. The owners of those forks are not accused of anything, and their pages say
  so.
- Files removed on this basis are held for **30 days** before they are destroyed, so that
  a mistake can be undone. After 30 days it cannot.
- One person adjudicates, on a best-effort basis. **No service level is promised.** Appeal
  is to the same person.
- Bytes are deleted; rows are not. A removed programme keeps its page and its place in the
  ancestry chain, so that a link somebody already holds does not rot.
